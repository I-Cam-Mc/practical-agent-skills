# Voice Profile Builder

Build a compact, evidence-linked voice guide that another agent can actually use.

## Establish authority and scope

- Confirm whose voice is being modelled and that the requester is authorised to use the samples.
- Ask what contexts the guide should cover, such as email, social posts, proposals or internal notes.
- Keep private samples local. Do not publish, quote or retain them outside the agreed workspace.
- Decline deceptive impersonation. A style guide should support the authorised writer, not misrepresent authorship to others.

## Analyse the samples

Use several representative samples when available. Separate:

- directly stated preferences;
- recurring observed patterns;
- context-specific variation;
- tentative inference;
- corrections the person has explicitly made.

Look for decision-relevant patterns in sentence length, rhythm, openings, directness, warmth, humour, vocabulary, formatting, evidence language, disagreement, calls to action and sign-offs. Do not turn one unusual sample into a universal rule.

## Produce the guide

Keep it short enough to load routinely. Include:

1. voice summary;
2. stable principles;
3. context-specific adjustments;
4. preferred and avoided constructions;
5. evidence and uncertainty notes;
6. a small set of synthetic before-and-after examples;
7. a correction loop for future drafts.

Do not include private names, contact details, confidential events or verbatim source passages. Synthetic examples should demonstrate the rule without recreating the original message.

## Validate

Draft one realistic new artefact, then ask the voice owner to mark what sounds right, wrong or overstated. Update the guide from corrections, not from self-scoring alone. Keep content accuracy and audience needs above stylistic mimicry.
